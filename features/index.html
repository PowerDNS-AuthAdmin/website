Features

Every feature of PowerDNS-AuthAdmin

Automatic PTR maintenance and a fleet-wide Reverse DNS report, Zone Monitoring from the outside, access control, single sign-on, safe record editing, an MCP server for AI assistants, multi-backend operations, DNSSEC and a real audit trail, in one self-hosted web UI for PowerDNS Authoritative.

Reverse DNS that keeps up with your records

Keep every A and AAAA record and its PTR in step on save, and see where they have drifted across the whole fleet.

PTR on save
Saving or deleting an A/AAAA record creates, updates or removes the matching PTR in a reverse zone hosted on any backend, including RFC 2317 classless zones. The review dialog lists the reverse changes before Save.
Global, per zone or per save
Switch it on for everything with the auto_ptr setting (Settings or provisioning), for one zone with X-AUTHADMIN-AUTO-PTR metadata, or for a single save with the editor's checkbox. The API and the MCP record tools take a ptr flag.
Never clobbers
Reverse zones are never created. A PTR that points at a different name is never replaced without an explicit takeover and never deleted, and the user needs record permissions in the reverse zone.
Reported and audited per address
Every PTR outcome comes back per address and is audited as record.ptr-sync next to the ordinary record changes on the reverse zone.
Fleet-wide report
Every A/AAAA record on every backend compared with its PTR: OK, missing, mismatch, ambiguous, orphan or not hosted. Record tables link each address to its PTR and each PTR back to its forward records, and the sidebar counts the addresses that need attention.
Rectify with a reviewed diff
Fix one row, a selection or everything fixable. Removals and bulk rectifies open Review changes with the PTR diff for each reverse zone before Apply.
Suggested fixes for orphans
A PTR with no forward record offers two fixes: add the forward record (the Add record wizard opens prefilled) or remove the PTR.
Public and internal kept apart
Matching follows the zone horizon, so public records only match public reverse zones and internal ones only internal. An unclassified reverse zone over private address space counts as internal.
Addresses you don't host
When no hosted reverse zone covers an address, its PTR is looked up through the public or internal resolvers, so you can see whether the upstream record matches.
Opt-in
Nothing is written until you turn on auto_ptr, set the zone metadata or tick the checkbox on a save. The Reverse DNS report needs REVERSE_DNS_ENABLED=true; while it is off, its page, routes and MCP tools do not exist. Feature reference

Zone Monitoring, from the outside in

A zone PowerDNS serves correctly can still be broken for everyone else. AuthAdmin checks every hosted zone the way the internet sees it.

Delegation from the root
The parent's NS set, glue and DS are read by walking down from the root servers, not through a recursive resolver, and compared with the zone's apex NS and DNSKEYs.
Every nameserver probed
Each NS address is asked for the SOA without recursion: did it answer, with the AA flag, with which serial, and does it serve the same NS set as PowerDNS.
Open zone transfer
An unsigned AXFR is tried against the zone's own nameservers only, and reading stops at the first message.
DNSSEC chain
The DS at the parent is compared with the zone's DNSKEYs by key tag and algorithm: a signed zone without a DS, a DS for an unsigned zone, or a DS left over from an unfinished key rollover.
Resolver view and SOA sanity
The configured public or internal resolvers are asked what they answer, and SOA timers, MNAME and RNAME are checked against the RFCs.
Fix with a reviewed diff
The "SOA MNAME is not one of the nameservers" finding suggests the zone's first nameserver. Fix shows the SOA before and after, saves through the zone's own SOA route and re-checks the zone.
Findings in the bell
Findings have stable codes and an error, warn or info severity with what to do. Errors and warnings reach the notification bell after two sightings, at once after a manual re-check, and the sidebar shows a count.
Horizon-aware
Internal zones skip the root walk and are judged against your internal resolvers, so a zone that is not meant to be on the internet is not flagged for it.
Single nameserver by design
A standalone backend can accept single-nameserver zones: the finding stays on the page as information and out of the bell.
Bounded and opt-in
Three zones at a time, spaced across the interval, 3 second timeouts and rate-limited manual re-checks. Off unless ZONE_MONITOR_ENABLED=true, because it sends DNS queries outside your network. Feature reference

Access control that fits real teams

Give each person exactly the zones and actions they need, and nothing that could break the zone.

~60 permissions
A typed vocabulary across zones, records, SOA, DNSSEC, metadata, TSIG, autoprimaries, templates, users, teams, roles, servers, API tokens, audit, settings and auth providers.
System and custom roles
Super Admin, Team Owner, Operator, Zone Editor and Read Only are seeded. Define your own roles in the UI or in provisioning.yaml.
Scoped assignments
Every grant is (user, role, scope) where scope is global, a team, a single zone or a single server, assigned in Admin → Users or by an identity provider group mapping. An operator can edit records in one zone and nowhere else.
Zone authority kept apart
The SOA, the apex NS RRset and zone settings each answer to their own permission, so a hosting customer can manage records without seeing the knobs that break the zone.
Teams
Group users and zones into teams and delegate administration to a Team Owner scoped to that team.
MFA by role
Mark a role as MFA-required and anyone holding it must enrol TOTP or a passkey.

Sign-in for every identity stack

Bring your existing identity provider. Groups map to roles on every sign-in.

OIDC
Generic OpenID Connect with PKCE, per-provider group → role mapping and RP-initiated logout that signs you out at the IdP too. OIDC guide
SAML 2.0
Works with AD FS, Authentik, Keycloak, Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, PingFederate and Shibboleth. Signed assertions required by default, encryption optional. SAML guide
LDAP / Active Directory
Direct bind against Active Directory or OpenLDAP with StartTLS, no federation broker in the path. Groups feed the same mapping as OIDC. LDAP guide
Passkeys and security keys
WebAuthn passkeys (Touch ID, Windows Hello, Android, iCloud Keychain, 1Password, Bitwarden) and hardware keys such as YubiKey, for passwordless sign-in or as a second factor. Passkeys guide
Local accounts and TOTP
Argon2id password hashing with OWASP parameters, TOTP authenticator apps, session management and sign-in rate limiting.
Scoped API tokens
Personal access tokens prefixed pda_pat_ carry their own permission scopes for automation and CI.

Zones and records, edited safely

See exactly what will change before it is written to PowerDNS.

Diff-before-apply
Every change is previewed as a BIND-style before / after diff, then applied as a single RRset PATCH.
Add record wizard
Add record next to Add zone picks the horizon, finds the zone with a type-ahead across every backend, checks live whether the name is already taken, shows the diff, then waits until every secondary or cluster peer serves the change.
Inline editing
Enter on a row, a double-click or the row's Edit button opens an edit strip under the record for TTL, value and the disabled flag. Renames and type changes escalate to the full editor with the draft carried along.
Keyboard-first
/ searches, N adds, arrows move, Enter edits in place, E opens the full editor, Delete deletes. Every select menu is a combo box with type-ahead and a filter for long lists.
Per-type validation
Structured editors per record type with inline validation and warnings, including trailing-dot fixes, CAA tags and underscore labels for DKIM, DMARC and SRV.
Optimistic concurrency
Edits are checked against the RRset you loaded, so two people can't silently overwrite each other.
Zone templates and cloning
Templates carry NS, SOA timers, prelude records, zone settings and per-kind metadata. Clone an existing zone in one step.
Configurable default TTL
New records start from the zone's metadata, the global setting, then 3600, and the editor tells you which applied.
Split-horizon zones
Mark a zone's internal copy so public and internal zones of the same name list separately with a clear badge.
BIND import and export
Import one or many zones from a BIND zone file, or export any selection as a single BIND-format bundle.
Change log
Every zone has a Change log tab built from the audit log, with diffs, and Show all changes opens the fleet-wide Change log narrowed to that zone.

Talk to your DNS with any AI assistant

A Model Context Protocol (MCP) server is built in. Describe the change; the assistant makes it through the same guard rails as the UI.

Built-in MCP server
POST /api/mcp speaks Streamable HTTP, stateless and JSON, so it works behind any reverse proxy and across replicas. MCP guide
Works with your client
Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Windsurf and any HTTP-capable MCP client connect with a URL and a Bearer header. Stdio-only clients bridge through mcp-remote.
Twenty task-shaped tools
Backends, zones and records, plus everything the new pages do: find a zone, check a name before adding it, wait for a zone to sync, read and rectify the Reverse DNS report, read and re-run Zone Monitoring, search, and list or dismiss notifications. The Reverse DNS and Zone Monitoring tools exist only while those features are on.
Never more than the token
Every call runs as the owner of a personal access token. Scopes and per-zone grants are the ceiling, re-evaluated on every call, and revocation is immediate.
Read-only tokens, read-only server
Write tools are only advertised to tokens that can write somewhere, so a look-only token never tempts the assistant to try.
Same validators, same diff path
Writes go through the per-type validators, optimistic concurrency and NOTIFY the UI uses. A malformed value comes back as a readable error the assistant can correct.
Destructive calls flagged
Replace, delete-records and delete-zone are annotated as destructive, so clients that confirm such calls will ask you first. Deleting a zone also requires the assistant to repeat its name.
Audited by name
Everything an assistant does lands in the audit log under the token owner, with the client's user agent, so you can filter for it.

Every PowerDNS topology, one UI

Run many PowerDNS Authoritative backends from a single install, side by side.

Three topologies
Standalone primaries, primary + secondaries groups (bootstrapped via autoprimary and NOTIFY / AXFR) and multi-primary clusters on a replicated database.
Amalgamated zone list
Zones from every backend merge into one searchable, sortable list with serial, DNSSEC and sync state per row.
Cluster peer selection
Route reads and writes by round-robin, random, lowest latency or least load.
Sync probes
Compare each secondary's serial with the primary, or each cluster peer with the highest-serial peer, and diff record-for-record on demand.
Catalog zones
Catalog zones (RFC 9432) are provisioning plumbing, so they sit on their own System tab with a member count, explain themselves on their own page and are read-only where PowerDNS generates them. Deleting a catalog that still has members needs a typed confirmation, a zone's catalog is set in its settings or at creation, and a secondary that doesn't consume the catalog never counts as out of sync. Needs PowerDNS 4.7 or later. Feature reference
PowerDNS 5.1 ready
PowerDNS 5.1 locks LUA records unless enable-lua-record-updates=yes. AuthAdmin reads the setting from each daemon, stops offering LUA where it is off and names the setting to change.
Read-only backends
Mark a backend read-only and every write route refuses it, for public nameservers fed by database replication.
Health advisories
Bell-driven alerts for unreachable hosts, replication drift, missing TSIG keys on secondaries and daemon config drift.
Notifications page
The bell previews the three most pressing notifications with a count by severity. Show all lists every backend health and Zone Monitoring notification with filters, search, detail and per-row or bulk dismissal, each visible only to users who can read what it is about.
Request log
Every HTTP call to PowerDNS is recorded so you can see what the app asked for and what came back, pruned after a configurable number of days.
SSRF guard
Backend URLs are validated; private networks and plain http are opt-in for trusted LANs.

DNSSEC, TSIG and metadata

The PowerDNS features you'd otherwise reach for pdnsutil to manage, with the secondaries kept in step.

Enable, disable, rectify
Sign a zone the way PowerDNS intends: one call adds default keys, rectifies and bumps the serial, then NOTIFYs. Disabling asks for the zone name and reminds you to pull the DS first.
The right DS set
The DNSSEC tab shows signing state, NSEC / NSEC3, SOA-EDIT and the DS records to publish, leaving out SHA-1 and GOST digests that registrars refuse.
Presigned secondaries handled
Transferred zones get SOA-EDIT so secondaries re-transfer fresh signatures, sync probes compare the served serial, and warnings flag LUA or ALIAS records a secondary cannot sign.
DNSSEC cryptokeys
Create, update and delete cryptokeys; creating one rectifies the zone so denial of existence is never left broken. Each key's activity is pulled from the audit log.
DNSSEC over the API
Signing state, keys, the DS set and per-mirror sync are readable with a scoped token, never private key material. Rollout order and curl examples
TSIG keys
Create and manage TSIG keys; revealing a secret is a separate permission from reading the key list.
Autoprimaries
Register and manage autoprimary (supermaster) entries for automatic secondary provisioning.
Zone metadata
Edit per-zone metadata with validation and diff history.

An audit trail you can trust

Answer “who changed what, when, and from where” without grepping logs.

Append-only
Every state-changing action is written once and never edited.
Before / after snapshots
Full snapshots with known secret fields redacted, plus the request ID and source IP.
PowerDNS HTTP trail
Each audit row links to the PowerDNS requests it caused.
Filters and CSV export
Filter by action, actor, resource and time, bookmark the query, and export to CSV.
Fleet-wide Change log
What people changed, everywhere: records, zones, DNSSEC, users and access, and configuration, with a Zone column and a Category filter. It sits in a Logs sidebar section next to the Request log and the Audit log.

Built to operate

Observable, scriptable and quiet. Nothing leaves your network unless you turn on the checks that look at it from outside.

First-boot provisioning
One provisioning.yaml sets up settings, roles, teams, templates, clusters, servers, demo zones and identity providers. Provisioning guide
Full API
Every admin surface has an /api/admin route that accepts a session or a scoped API token.
Metrics and probes
Prometheus /metrics, /healthz liveness and /readyz readiness gated on the database and migration version.
Backup and restore
Export the app database (users, roles, grants, backends, providers, settings, audit log) as JSON with secrets still encrypted, and merge it back into a fresh install that shares the encryption key.
Structured logs
Pino JSON logs with secrets redacted.
Global search
A header palette (Ctrl K / ⌘K or /) fuzzy-matches zones, records and config, with the PowerDNS request log and the audit log in their own tabs. Results show where a zone or record lives and its state, every source is filtered by your permissions, and secrets are never indexed.
Realtime UI
A server-sent events bus pushes external zone edits to open browsers without a refresh, and the stream reopens on its own after a tab sleeps or the network drops, without reloading the page.
High availability
Postgres plus Redis for multiple replicas. Migrations are serialised by an advisory lock, so rolling boots are safe.
No telemetry, no CDN
No phone-home and no third-party assets, so air-gapped installs are first-class. Zone Monitoring and the Reverse DNS lookups are the only features that send DNS queries out, and both are off by default.
Hardened by default
Per-request CSP nonces, CSRF double-submit, AES-256-GCM encryption of stored secrets and a published security policy. Hardening guide

Mobile-first, accessible, light and dark

Every page reflows down to a phone and works without a mouse, so you can fix a record from wherever you are.

Responsive everywhere
An off-canvas drawer, stacked tables and full-width dialogs on small screens.
Keyboard operable
Sortable headers are real buttons, pickers are combo boxes, dialogs trap and restore focus, and the mobile drawer is inert while closed.
WCAG AA contrast
Semantic colours used as text meet AA in both themes, every field is labelled for screen readers, and animation honours reduced-motion preferences.
Light and dark themes
Follows your system preference, with a one-tap toggle. Self-hosted Inter and JetBrains Mono, no font CDN.
Live status
A header chip shows backend connectivity and sync state at a glance, naming the actual state: lagging, missing or error.
